Singapore Employment Act Guide 2026: Coverage and Pay Rules

Updated and source-checked 2026-11-07. Independent educational guide by SG Salary Pro.

Start with your job duties and contract before choosing a pay formula. Employment Act coverage and Part 4 coverage answer different questions: being outside Part 4 does not automatically remove all Employment Act rights.

Check coverage in two steps

The Act generally covers local and foreign employees under a contract of service. Exceptions include domestic workers, seafarers, civil servants and statutory board employees. Part 4 adds rules on hours, rest days and overtime for eligible workmen with monthly basic pay up to S$4,500 and non-workmen up to S$2,600; managers and executives are excluded.

Use MOM's coverage explanation to check duties, rather than relying on the job title alone. These are the thresholds verified for this guide; they are not presented as a new 2026 increase.

Example: basic pay and allowances are separate

Assume a non-managerial clerk receives S$2,500 basic pay plus a S$300 allowance. Monthly cash before deductions is S$2,800, but the Part 4 salary test uses S$2,500 basic pay. If the duties meet the non-workman definition, that basic amount falls within the S$2,600 threshold. A manager with the same pay needs a different coverage assessment.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not use gross pay for a basic-pay coverage test, assume every person called a manager has identical duties, or interpret a tool result as a ruling on a disputed employment contract.

Frequently asked question

Does earning above S$2,600 remove all employment protection?

No. The figure concerns the non-workman Part 4 test. Check the Act's general coverage separately, then the rule relevant to your issue.
