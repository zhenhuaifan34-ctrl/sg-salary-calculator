Singapore Salary Benchmark Guide 2026: Compare Like for Like

A useful salary benchmark matches occupation, responsibility, population, reference year and wage definition. Start with the MOM occupational wage source and coverage, then read the notes attached to the exact table you use.

Choose the right denominator

MOM occupational wages and the national income measure answer different questions. Occupational wage statistics support job comparisons; an overall resident median includes a wider mix of jobs. The income summary explicitly labels a measure including employer CPF and one-twelfth of annual bonus. Do not compare that amount directly with a basic-salary figure.

The June 2024 open dataset covers full-time Singapore Citizen and Permanent Resident employees in private establishments with at least 25 employees. Occupational gross monthly wages include regular cash components such as overtime, commissions and allowances before employee CPF and income tax. They exclude employer CPF, bonuses and lump sums. Basic wages exclude those regular extras too.

The Explorer uses the June 2024 open dataset. MOM also published the June 2025 occupational wage tables on 30 June 2026. Check the reference year on each comparison.

Read one occupation’s wage distribution

In the June 2024 MOM open dataset, software developers had gross monthly wages of S$6,658 at P25, S$8,888 at the median and S$13,513 at P75. These published values describe full-time resident employees in the survey’s covered establishments. Use the software developer distribution to see the same figures and compare a like-for-like monthly wage.

Example: same base, different package

Two roles both pay S$5,000 basic monthly. Role A promises one month's AWS; Role B has none. With no bonus or allowances, annual cash is 5,000 × 13 = S$65,000 for A and 5,000 × 12 = S$60,000 for B. Their basic-pay comparison is identical while the annual-package comparison is not. AWS must be confirmed in the terms.

A repeatable evidence checklist

  1. Record the source, table year and date accessed.
  2. Match occupation duties and level; a title alone is insufficient.
  3. Read whether pay is basic or gross, includes CPF, includes bonuses, and covers residents or a different population.
  4. Keep a range and note sample limitations; survey aggregates cannot determine one individual's deserved pay.

Common mistakes

Comparing take-home pay with gross pay, adding employer CPF twice, treating a recruitment range as a guaranteed offer, or inventing a precise 2026 market median from a different year's data. This guide does not publish a new salary database.

Frequently asked question

Does being below a median prove I am underpaid?

No. A median locates the middle of its measured group. Review your actual duties, level, hours and the group's coverage before drawing a conclusion.
