Is a 10% Salary Increase Worth Switching Jobs in Singapore?

A 10% monthly raise is a calculation, not a universal switching rule. Test whether the percentage survives changes in guaranteed annual cash, variable bonus and hours. For the later exit-cost and payback decision, use the switching-cost guide.

Last updated 8 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Test the 10% monthly raise against guaranteed annual cash.
  • Review variable bonus and work hours in separate scenarios.
  • Use the separate payback guide to examine transition costs after checking the package.

Three ways the same 10% raise can look different

The third row assumes the current S$2,000 payment is genuinely confirmed, not a hoped-for bonus. MOM says AWS and bonus payments depend on the contract or collective agreement; verify each amount before including it.

Test hours separately

If annual pay rises by 10% but annual work time rises by 15%, relative pay per work hour is 1.10 ÷ 1.15 = 95.65% of the old level, a 4.35% reduction. Commute time can add another burden. This is a comparison assumption, not a statutory hourly wage formula.

Use a base case and a downside case

In the Offer Comparison Calculator, first enter guaranteed cash only. Run a second case with an explicitly labelled bonus assumption. Then use the Job Switch Calculator for the current-job decision and the Salary Calculator to check applicable CPF cash effects.

Common mistakes

Assuming every 10% raise beats lost AWS, applying full CPF rates without checking PR year or ceilings, counting uncertain bonus as guaranteed, or deciding from one percentage before reading the offer's hours and notice terms.

Frequently asked question

Should I demand 15% or 20% instead?

There is no universal minimum in this guide. Use comparable-role evidence from the benchmark guide and identify the package and time changes that your requested amount must cover.
