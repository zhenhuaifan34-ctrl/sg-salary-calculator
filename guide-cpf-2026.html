Singapore CPF Guide 2026: Rates, Wage Ceilings and Cash Pay

CPF cash deductions depend on age, citizenship or PR year, wage type and wage amount. The common 20% employee rate is one case, rather than a rate for every worker.

Use the Salary Calculator for a salary estimate within its displayed scope. Check CPF Board's tables for payroll, graduated PR rates, low wages and birthdays that change an age band.

Last updated 8 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Match the wage month, age band and PR status to CPF Board’s contribution table.
  • Separate Ordinary Wages from Additional Wages when checking the ceilings.
  • Employee CPF reduces cash pay. Employer CPF is paid on top.

2026 rates for full-rate employees

For Singapore Citizens and PRs from the third year onward, with monthly wages above S$750:

2026 employee and employer contribution rates

55 and below20%17%
Above 55 to 6018%16%
Above 60 to 6512.5%12.5%
Above 65 to 707.5%9%
Above 705%7.5%

First- and second-year PRs and monthly wages of S$750 or less require separate tables. Foreign employees generally do not receive CPF contributions.

For wages earned from 1 January 2027, CPF Board has announced higher rates for two age bands. At full rates for monthly wages above S$750, employees above 55 to 60 contribute 19% and employers 16.5%. For employees above 60 to 65, both contribute 13%. CPF Board provides separate tables for lower wages, and the graduated rates for first- and second-year PRs are unchanged. Use the table for the year in which the wages were earned. CPF Board's 2027 contribution rates

Pick the right month and age band

For the same eligible full-rate employee, use the 2026 rates for wages earned in December 2026 and the announced 2027 rates for wages earned in January 2027. For a birthday that moves an employee into a new band, CPF Board applies the new rate from the next month. A 55th birthday in August means the above-55 band starts in September. For graduated PR rates, check the applicable table in the official CPF calculator.

Example: S$4,000 of Ordinary Wages

Assume a 35-year-old Singapore Citizen, S$4,000 monthly Ordinary Wages, no Additional Wages, and no other deductions. Employee CPF is S$800; employer CPF is S$680; total CPF is S$1,480. Cash salary after employee CPF is S$3,200. The S$680 employer share is paid on top, not deducted from that cash salary. Income tax and other deductions are separate.

Ordinary Wages and bonus ceilings

The monthly Ordinary Wage ceiling is S$8,000 from 1 January 2026. The annual salary ceiling remains S$102,000. The Additional Wage ceiling is S$102,000 less the year's Ordinary Wages subject to CPF. An annual bonus is not simply capped at S$8,000.

For example, twelve months of CPF-subject OW at S$8,000 total S$96,000, leaving S$6,000 of AW headroom. A S$10,000 bonus would not all fit within that headroom. Verify the annual adjustment with CPF Board.

Common errors and next steps

Do not subtract employer CPF from cash, apply full rates to every PR, or treat CPF as income tax. Compare annual packages using the Offer Comparison Calculator; read the income tax guide for tax residency and chargeable income.

Frequently asked question

Does CPF after a bonus use the same ceiling as monthly salary?

No. Identify OW and AW first, then use their respective ceilings and CPF Board's contribution rounding.
