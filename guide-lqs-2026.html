Singapore LQS Guide 2026: S$1,800 Gross and Part-Time Rules

The Local Qualifying Salary (LQS) increased to S$1,800 on 1 July 2026. It is a gross-wage requirement in the relevant firms, not a universal S$1,800 basic-wage rule for every job.

Last updated 8 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • LQS wage compliance and the foreign-worker quota count are separate tests.
  • Check whether a Progressive Wage Model applies to the worker.
  • Use MOM’s wage schedule for a full-time employee who works beyond 44 hours a week.

Wage compliance and quota counting are separate

Firms hiring foreign workers must pay applicable Progressive Wage Model (PWM) wages to covered locals and at least LQS to locals outside those PWMs. The quota-count thresholds do not excuse underpaying a full-time employee.

Local Qualifying Salary by work arrangement
Employee arrangementCurrent requirement
Local part-timer, below 35 hours/weekAt least S$10.50 gross per hour
Full-time local, 35–44 hours/weekAt least S$1,800 gross per month
Full-time local above 44 hours/weekHigher total requirement; use MOM's LQS wage schedule

For quota counting, an eligible local earning at least S$1,800 monthly counts as one; S$900 to below S$1,800 counts as 0.5. Read MOM's full counting rules and use its official quota calculator for the firm's entitlement.

Example: a compliant part-timer can be a half count

Assume a local works fewer than 35 hours a week, totals 100 paid hours in the month and receives S$1,050 gross wages. The hourly test is 1,050 ÷ 100 = S$10.50. Monthly wages lie between S$900 and S$1,800, so the wage meets the stated hourly requirement while the monthly quota count is 0.5, subject to the other eligibility conditions.

What to check on your payslip

Keep basic pay, allowances, ordinary hours and OT distinguishable. Do not subtract employee CPF before testing gross wages. Where PWM applies, check its sector or occupation requirements first; meeting LQS alone does not replace a higher applicable requirement.

The Salary Calculator helps inspect stated salary and CPF assumptions; it does not certify LQS/PWM compliance or a foreign-worker quota. Use the official wage schedule and Progressive Wage Portal linked from MOM for that check.

Use the requirement for the pay period

For the full-time LQS threshold, the change from S$1,600 to S$1,800 took effect on 1 July 2026. Check the period on the payslip and compare any week above 44 hours with MOM’s LQS wage schedule. MOM also links to the Progressive Wage Portal for checking wage details.

Common mistake and question

Can a full-time local be paid S$1,000 because the employee counts as half?

The quota count and wage-compliance tests must both be checked. A half-count threshold is not permission to ignore the applicable full-time LQS or PWM wage requirement.
