Singapore Overtime Guide 2026: Eligibility, Formula and Example

Check statutory coverage before multiplying salary by an overtime rate. Use the Salary Calculator within its stated scope, with basic salary and eligible OT hours prepared separately.

Last updated 8 November 2026

Key takeaways

  • Check Part 4 coverage using actual duties and monthly basic pay.
  • Classify the workday, rest day or public holiday before estimating pay.
  • Check the separate overtime-hour limit and payment deadline.

Who can claim statutory overtime?

MOM's monthly-rated calculator covers non-workmen with basic pay up to S$2,600 and workmen with basic pay up to S$4,500 under Part 4. Managers and executives are excluded. A non-workman above S$2,600 does not become statutorily eligible merely by capping salary at S$2,600; check the contract instead.

Check your hours in this order

  1. Confirm the work category and basic salary used for Part 4.
  2. Identify the rostered day type and normal hours before counting excess hours.
  3. Record the date and hours so you can reconcile the overtime entry with payroll.

Formula and a ten-hour example

For a monthly-rated employee, hourly basic rate = 12 × monthly basic rate ÷ (52 × 44). Statutory OT pay is at least 1.5 × hourly basic rate × OT hours.

Assume an eligible non-managerial employee has basic pay S$2,288 and ten qualifying ordinary-day OT hours. Hourly basic rate = 12 × 2,288 ÷ 2,288 = S$12. Minimum OT = 12 × 1.5 × 10 = S$180. Basic pay plus that OT is S$2,468 before CPF, other wages and deductions. The numbers are chosen to avoid an intermediate-rounding ambiguity.

Limits are separate from the amount owed

The normal maximum is 72 OT hours a month, with MOM exemptions possible. Rest-day or public-holiday work within usual daily hours is excluded from this limit; additional hours beyond those usual hours count. Confirm shift arrangements and exceptions on MOM's page. OT pay is due within 14 days after the salary period ends.

Do not turn every non-working day into a multiplier

A rostered rest day, a non-working day and a public holiday are different categories. Record which day was worked, who requested it and the hours before using the relevant official calculation. Read the public-holiday guide for holiday scenarios.

Common mistakes

Using gross pay including allowances as basic pay, counting meal breaks as work, using a simple 2× rule for every Sunday, or assuming a calculator confirms legal eligibility.

Frequently asked question

Can a contractual OT benefit exist outside Part 4?

Check the written employment terms. A contractual arrangement is separate from the statutory minimum described here.
